Anaerobic Silt & Sludge

Decomposing organic silt entering through municipal or borewell mains creates an oxygen-depleted bottom layer giving off foul rotten-egg hydrogen sulfide smells.

Risk: Severe Pipe Clogging

Coliform & E. Coli

Carried in through rainwater cracks or unsealed lids by birds, rodents, and airborne spores. Causes severe gastroenteritis, dysentery, and viral fever.

Risk: Skin Rash & Eye Irritation

Quick 30-Second Self Check

Is Your Rooftop Tank Due for Immediate Mechanized Sanitization?

If you check 2 or more of the following warning signs, your domestic water supply is currently compromised by biofilm or sludge.

Discolored First Morning Water

Yellowish or brownish tint when opening the tap after 6 hours of overnight stagnation.

Earthy or Musty Odor

Unpleasant smell when showering or washing vegetables, caused by anaerobic silt decomposition.

Skin Itching Post-Shower

Unexplained dryness, scalp irritation, or red spots after warm bathing in high-sediment water.

Declining Shower Pressure

Aerator screens and mixer valves choking on rust flakes and hardened calcium fragments.

Over 6 Months Since Last Wash

Municipal water boards mandate high-pressure de-sludging at least every 90 to 180 days.

Damaged or Unsealed Lid

Airborne bird droppings, insect larvae, and dust enter through warped or cracked manhole caps.

Critical Truths

Common Tank Water Myths Debunked

Dangerous misconceptions that leave families exposed to contaminated bathing and cooking water.

✕

Myth: "My kitchen RO purifier protects us, so the overhead tank doesn't matter."

Fact: You only drink 2 to 3 liters per day, but you absorb up to 60 liters during hot showers, toothbrushing, and food prep. High bacterial loads in your tank cause skin rashes, vaginal yeast infections, and eye inflammation regardless of your kitchen filter.

✕

Myth: "If the water looks clear in the glass, my tank is 100% clean."

Fact: Heavy silt and anaerobic biofilms stick to the bottom and side walls without clouding the top layer of water. The moment the water level drops below 30%, these concentrated pathogens are sucked directly into your home plumbing.

✕

Myth: "Pouring a bottle of Dettol or bleaching powder once a year is enough."

Fact: Household antiseptics are not potable water disinfectants. They react with organic sludge to produce carcinogenic chloramines. Only high-pressure mechanized vacuuming followed by physical UV-C radiation achieves safe, chemical-free sterilization.
